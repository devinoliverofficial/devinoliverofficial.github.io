This Privacy Policy explains how SHYBEAST LLC (“we,” “us,” or “our”) collects, uses, and shares information when you use the Greenroom app (on the web and on iPhone), website, and related software and services (the “App”). If you have questions, email us at greenroom.tourbook@gmail.com.

In short: Greenroom keeps what you put into it and what it needs to run your tours. We do not sell your information, we do not show ads, and we do not use advertising or analytics trackers.

1. Information We Collect

Information you give us

  • Account information: your name, email address, password, @username, date of birth, and phone number. We keep your date of birth so we can check that you are 18 or older, and only you can see it in the App. Your password is stored in scrambled form by our login service, and we cannot read it.
  • Profile: your photo, a short bio, the roles you have on tour, the artists you say you have toured with, and what brings you to Greenroom (a band member, crew, a fan, or a promoter).
  • Tour information: what you or other users put into the App about a tour, such as show dates, venues, day sheets, budgets, expenses, income, settlements, notes, supplies, to-do lists, bus check-ins, and crew names, roles, contact details, and pay.
  • Guest lists: the names of guests that people add for a show and, when they are given, an email address, phone number, and ticket type.
  • Messages: direct messages, tour chat, notes that come with flowers, special requests, and what you ask Ari.
  • Shows and ratings: shows you mark as attended, ratings and reviews of shows and venues, the artists and people you follow, flowers you give or get, and endorsements.
  • Statements: when you upload a statement, Greenroom reads it and keeps only the charges (date, amount, and store), never the file and never its account number. A file your phone can read by itself stays on your phone; otherwise its text or pages are sent to Anthropic to be read (see Section 3), and we do not keep them.
  • Flyers and settlements: show flyers you add to a tour or an artist page are kept and shown there. When a settlement email or a photo of a settlement is read, we keep the numbers and notes taken from it, and, for an email, a log line with who sent it and its subject, not the email itself.
  • Linked bank cards, Square, and setlist.fm: see Section 5 for bank cards through Plaid. If a Manager connects a Square account for merch, we keep the access key Square gives us and read that account’s sales and payouts. If you add a setlist.fm key for an artist page, we keep it to read that artist’s history.
  • Information about other people: details about crew members, guests, and other people you add to a tour or invite, such as their names and email addresses.
  • Messages and feedback to us: anything you send us, such as bug reports and suggestions.

Information we collect automatically

  • Agreement records: when you accepted our agreements, which versions you accepted, your email address at the time, the IP address you used, and a description of your device.
  • Error reports: when something goes wrong in the App, it can send us a short report with the screen, an error code, your account, and the type of device.
  • Codes and invitations: which Greenroom code or tour invitation you used to join.
  • Server records: like most services, our servers see the IP address and device type of the requests they receive.
  • Phone alerts: if you turn on alerts, we keep your phone’s alert address and your choices about which alerts you want. Alerts travel through Apple’s, Google’s, or Mozilla’s push service.
  • Location: if you allow your phone’s location so the App can show how far a place is, that is worked out on your phone. We do not receive it or keep it.
  • On your device: the App keeps your sign-in and settings on your device, and saved copies of the App’s own files so it opens quickly with a weak signal. We do not use advertising cookies or trackers, and we do not run analytics tools.

2. How We Use Information

We use information to:

  • run the App, including calculating your tour numbers, sorting expenses from statements, reading show dates from flyers, and showing tour information to the people you share it with;
  • show your profile as described in Section 4, and build the show history described in Section 6;
  • send you emails about your account: invitations, sign-in and sign-up codes, password resets, and important notices about the App or our agreements;
  • send you the phone alerts you have turned on;
  • keep the App secure, fix problems, review reports of abuse, and improve it;
  • enforce our agreements, including the Beta Tester Non-Disclosure Agreement, and protect our rights and the rights of others;
  • comply with the law; and
  • create de-identified, aggregated information that does not identify you.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not use your tour information for advertising.

We share information only:

  • With people on your tours. Anyone with access to a tour can see what their role allows, including information about you that other members add.
  • With other people in the App, as described in Section 4.
  • With the companies that help us run the App. Where they receive information from us, their use of it is limited by their agreements with us or their published terms.
    • Supabase stores everything the App keeps, runs our login and our server functions, and is hosted in the United States (Ohio).
    • GitHub puts the App’s website online and holds its code.
    • Anthropic reads flyers, statements, settlement emails and photos, the lines you ask about with More info, searches for the tours an artist has played, and Ari’s questions. It receives what is sent for that one request.
    • Plaid connects the bank cards you choose to link (Section 5), and Square connects a merch account if a Manager chooses to.
    • Brevo sends our emails, so it handles your email address and the message.
    • MusicBrainz, setlist.fm, Wikipedia, and Wikimedia Commons provide artist, show, and photo information (Section 6). When you search for an artist, the name you type and your IP address go from your phone to MusicBrainz or Wikipedia. Our servers also ask these sources, and OpenStreetMap-based place search, for information about artists, shows, and places.
    • Google Fonts supplies the App’s fonts and jsDelivr supplies one of its code libraries, so each sees your IP address when the App loads.
    • Apple distributes the iPhone app and carries alerts to iPhones, under Apple’s own terms.
  • For legal reasons, when we believe sharing is required by law, legal process, or a government request, or is needed to protect the rights, property, or safety of us, our users, or others, including to enforce our agreements.
  • In a business transfer, if SHYBEAST LLC or the App is sold, merged, or otherwise transferred.
  • With your permission.

Greenroom’s administrators can read any record when they need to run the App, fix a problem, review a report, or follow the law.

We may also share de-identified, aggregated information that does not identify you.

We do not show ads, and except for the service providers named above working for us, we do not allow other companies to collect information about your online activities over time and across other websites or apps when you use the App. We do not share personal information with other companies for their own marketing.

4. Your Profile, and Who Can Find You

  • Anyone signed in to Greenroom can search for you by your @username and will see your name, photo, and roles.
  • People who tour with you or share an artist page with you can open your full profile: your bio, and the artists and tours you have been on (names and dates, never money). People on a tour with you also see your phone number and email.
  • Your date of birth is seen by no one but you.
  • Direct messages are seen by you and the person you wrote to. If a message is reported, we keep a copy of it for the report.
  • You can block a person. Nobody is told. A blocked person can no longer open your profile, message you, or follow you.
  • Artist pages, their show history, and the people listed on them can be seen by anyone signed in to the App, and are kept as a lasting record, as Section 6 explains.

5. Linked Bank Cards (Plaid)

A Tour Manager can connect the bank or card company their tour cards are with, through Plaid, so the charges on those cards file themselves onto their tour. A card can also be linked for one person’s own spending only (see “Cards you link only for yourself” below). This section explains what that connection reads, what Greenroom keeps, and what it never touches. When you connect a bank, Plaid collects your bank sign-in on its own screen under its own privacy policy (plaid.com/legal).

What Greenroom can do

  • Read charges. That’s all. Greenroom asks Plaid for one thing only: transactions.
  • It cannot move money. Greenroom never asks for payments, transfers or anything else that moves money, and its server refuses every Plaid request except reading accounts and transactions and connecting or disconnecting a bank.

What Greenroom reads through Plaid

  • The names and types of your card, checking and savings accounts (for example “Business Gold Card”), so you can choose which ones feed your tour.
  • Transactions on those accounts: date, amount and merchant name. For an account you switch off, Greenroom keeps nothing about its lines. At most it counts how many there were, so it can tell you they were not looked at.
  • For each bank account you mark for income (merch, guarantees or both): the date and amount of its deposits, to confirm that what atVenu said a show would pay, or a show’s guarantee, actually arrived. Also one word for how each deposit came in (a check, a wire, a direct deposit, a transfer such as Zelle, or “other” when the bank’s line does not say), read from the bank’s line, so the App can set it beside how a show’s guarantee is paid. And one word for who sent it, from a short list (Live Nation or atVenu), only when the bank’s line says so plainly, so the App can say what such a deposit usually is. The line itself is not kept.
  • For each credit card you log: its balance when you start logging a tour, put on that tour under Credit card, and the payments you make on it afterwards. At each refresh Greenroom also reads the card’s current balance and shows it as Owed on cards. Only that one latest number is kept (each new reading replaces the last), and it never changes what the tour spent.

What Greenroom never reads or keeps

  • Your bank password. You sign in on Plaid’s screen or your bank’s own site, never inside Greenroom.
  • Full card numbers, account numbers or routing numbers. Greenroom keeps only an account’s last four digits, as part of its name, so you can tell your cards apart.
  • Bank account balances. Plaid includes them when it lists your accounts; Greenroom throws them away. The only balances kept are a credit card’s, as described above.
  • Money coming into your bank accounts. Of that, Greenroom keeps at most a count of how many lines there were, with three exceptions: deposits on accounts you mark for income (only each deposit’s date, amount, how it came in and who sent it, in the words described above); money coming back from something you sent (a returned Zelle, wire or payment), which is kept and shown in your pile the way a refund is; and a line Greenroom cannot read properly, which is kept with whatever could be read, so that it is not lost, but never when it is money coming into a bank account.

What Greenroom keeps

  • The key Plaid issues for each bank you connect. It is locked (encrypted) on Greenroom’s server, where only the server can open it. It is never sent to your phone or anyone else’s.
  • Spending it looks at: the date, amount, a cleaned-up merchant name, the account’s name and a category. Charges Greenroom isn’t sure about wait in your pile until they are added to a tour or set aside (who can see the pile is listed below). Refunds on a card wait in the same pile. For money sent, the bank’s own description is kept in place of a merchant name, with long numbers taken out, because it is the only thing that says who was paid. That covers a Zelle, a wire and a transfer, a cash withdrawal, a loan payment, money sent or advanced from a card, and money coming back from any of those. The description can carry a name, a phone number or an email address: the one the money went to, and sometimes your own.
  • Charges on your tours: once added, a charge is part of that tour’s budget.
  • Lines it leaves out of your pile: paying a credit card (and that payment arriving on the card), money moved to your own savings or investments, a line dated outside every tour’s card dates or from before a card’s opening balance, a line for $0, and a line your bank later takes back. Greenroom keeps them, each with the reason it was left out and the same details as any other line (date, amount, name, account), so that every line your bank sends is accounted for. You can see them in the App under “Left out and set aside” and bring one into your pile. None of them is counted on a tour unless you do.
  • Charges you set aside: kept, with the reason picked (not a tour charge, already typed in, or a reimbursement), when, and who on the tour did it, so they can be listed and put back.
  • A tally of each read: every time Greenroom reads a bank it notes when, which bank, and how many lines the bank sent, the days they span, and where they went: already here, waiting, left out, and, as a number only, lines on accounts you switched off (by the account’s name) and money coming in. There are no merchant names and no amounts in it. It is how Greenroom checks that no line went missing. The newest 400 are kept, and full checks for 30 days.

Everything is stored with Greenroom’s hosting provider, Supabase, which encrypts stored data.

Who can see it

  • The pile of charges waiting for a look on a tour: you, and that tour’s Tour Manager (a Manager on the tour whose tour role is Tour Manager), who can sort it. For an artist’s Off Tour book: you, and the Tour Manager of any of that artist’s tours. A line that is waiting for no tour in particular shows in every one of those piles, so each of those Tour Managers sees it too. That includes a line you bring in from “Left out and set aside” when it belongs to no tour. A line that was waiting on a tour you have since deleted shows in your own piles only, not in a Tour Manager’s.
  • The lines left out or set aside, while they stay there, and the tallies of each read: only you.
  • Charges on a tour: you, and the people you make Managers of that tour. People you invite as crew never receive the tour’s money at all: the database gives them a copy of the tour without it.
  • Charges on an artist’s Off Tour book: you, and the Managers of that artist’s tours.
  • Greenroom never sells your data or shares it with advertisers. It is used only to show your tours’ budgets, and it leaves Greenroom only in the one case described just below.

When you tap More info on a line you are sorting, that one line (its name, amount and date, and the name of its card or account without the digits), the names of the categories it could be filed under, and where the tour was in the days around it are sent to Anthropic, the company whose AI reads for Greenroom, to work out what the line was. If you then tap Look it up online, that service searches the web for the name. For money coming in, Greenroom keeps only the date, the amount, how it came in and who sent it, so what goes with those is what the App already says about that deposit and what the tour has logged for the nights around it. Nothing is sent until you tap, the answer is shown only to you, and nothing is filed, ticked or logged for you.

Cards you link only for yourself

A card you link for your own spending is kept apart from every tour. Its charges are seen by you only: not by a Tour Manager and not by anyone on your tours, and they do not count on a tour’s budget unless you yourself send one to a tour, for example to ask to be paid back. Everything above about what Greenroom reads and keeps applies to it too, and you disconnect it the same way.

How long it’s kept

  • A bank’s Plaid key: until you disconnect that bank.
  • Your pile (the lines still waiting to be sorted): until you sort them, or until you disconnect your last bank, which clears them.
  • The record of what’s already been brought in (so nothing comes in twice), the lines you set aside and the lines left out: they stay after you disconnect, until you delete your account.
  • The tallies of each read: the newest 400, and full checks for 30 days. All of them go when you delete your account.
  • Charges on a tour: until you remove them. Deleting a tour hides it, with its charges, for everyone on it; it is kept so you can restore it, and is not erased. Deleting your whole account erases the tours you made and had deleted.

Disconnecting a bank

  • In Greenroom: Settings, then Linked Cards, then the trash next to a bank, or Disconnect every bank. Greenroom tells Plaid to close the connection and deletes its key right away. Disconnecting your last bank also clears the lines still waiting to be sorted. Lines already on a tour, lines you set aside and lines left out are kept. While no bank is linked the App does not show the list of what was left out and set aside; it shows again when you link a bank.
  • Or through Plaid: at my.plaid.com you can see and remove the apps connected to your banks. Greenroom stops being able to read anything right away.
  • Remove a charge, or a whole import, from a tour to erase it from that tour.

Greenroom is an independent app. It is not made, endorsed or supported by Plaid or your bank.

6. Where Show History Comes From

Artist pages show a history of shows and tours: dates, venues, cities, and tour names. Users do not type this history in. Greenroom builds it from public sources: setlist.fm, MusicBrainz, the bands’ own old web pages as saved by the Internet Archive’s Wayback Machine, press and festival websites, and Wikipedia. Where a source asks for a credit, the page shows it, for example “Tour data: setlist.fm.” Pictures of artists are uploaded by the artist, or come from Wikimedia Commons with credit.

People who were on a tour can confirm it for their own profile, and the artist page approves it. This history is meant to be a lasting record.

If something about an artist, a venue, or a show is wrong, or you are the artist, the venue, or the person it is about and want it corrected or taken down, email greenroom.tourbook@gmail.com and tell us what it is and where you saw it.

7. How Long We Keep Information, and Deleting Your Account

We keep information while your account is active and for as long as we need it to run the App.

You can delete your account in the App: open the menu, tap Settings, then Delete my account. We then delete your profile, date of birth, photo, direct messages, flowers, follows, endorsements, claims, alerts, linked bank keys, merch and setlist.fm connections, shows you marked, and the tours you made and had deleted, and you are removed from the tours you were on. A tour you still run has to be deleted first, and an artist page is kept as a permanent record, so an account that runs one stays until you email us.

What can remain after you delete your account:

  • the record that you accepted our agreements (your email address, when, the versions, your IP address, and your device description), so we can show that you did;
  • a copy of anything that was reported, kept with the report for safety, with the names as they were then;
  • what the person who runs a tour typed into their own tour, such as a crew list or pay, which stays in that tour’s records;
  • the public show history on artist pages, which does not come from you;
  • backup copies of our database, which are kept for about a week and then replaced; and
  • information we must keep to comply with the law, resolve disputes, or enforce our agreements.

8. Security

We use reasonable safeguards to protect your information, but no system is completely secure, so we cannot guarantee it. The App does not need full card or bank account numbers or your bank passwords, so please do not upload them. If a security incident affects your personal information, we will notify you as the law requires.
